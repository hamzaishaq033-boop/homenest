Food Storage Containers: What to Look For Before Buying

The right food storage containers can make your kitchen easier to organize and everyday leftovers simpler to manage. Before buying a large set, consider the material, size, lids, storage space and how you actually use them.

By HomeNest Editorial Team • Published September 22, 2026 • Updated September 22, 2026

Food storage containers come in many shapes, sizes and materials. While a large matching set may look appealing, the most useful containers are the ones that fit your kitchen, your refrigerator and the types of food you regularly store.

Here are the main things to consider before choosing food storage containers for your home.

1. Consider the Container Material

Common options include glass, plastic and other food-contact materials designed for storage. Each option has different practical characteristics, so consider how you plan to use the containers.

2. Choose the Right Sizes

A container set with several sizes can be more useful than a set where every container is nearly the same size.

Think about the portions and foods you normally store before choosing the sizes you need.

3. Check the Lid Design

A good lid should fit securely and be easy to open and close. Look for designs that are practical for your everyday routine.

4. Think About Refrigerator Space

The container should fit comfortably on your refrigerator shelves. Stackable designs can make better use of vertical space.

Before buying a large set, consider the shelf height and depth of your refrigerator.

5. Look for Stackable Shapes

Containers that stack neatly can reduce wasted cabinet and refrigerator space.

Square or rectangular shapes can also make it easier to organize shelves compared with containers that have irregular shapes.

6. Consider How Easy They Are to Clean

Food containers should be practical to clean after everyday use. Smooth surfaces and simple lid designs can make cleaning easier.

7. Check Dishwasher Compatibility

If you use a dishwasher, check the manufacturer's instructions to confirm whether the container and its lid are suitable for dishwasher cleaning.

8. Check Microwave Instructions

If you plan to reheat food in a container, check the manufacturer's instructions to confirm whether it is suitable for microwave use.

Do not assume that every container is designed for heating simply because it is marketed for food storage.

9. Look at the Shape

The shape can affect how efficiently a container uses your available storage space.

Rectangular containers can be convenient for refrigerator shelves, while smaller round containers may work well for certain foods.

10. Think About Portion Sizes

Smaller containers can be useful for individual portions, sauces or small leftovers, while larger containers may be better for family portions.

11. Consider Transparency

Clear containers can make it easier to see what is inside without opening every lid.

This can be especially useful when several containers are stored together in a refrigerator.

12. Look for Secure Storage

If you regularly carry food to work, school or another location, consider containers with lids designed to close securely.

Always follow the manufacturer's instructions for proper use.

13. Avoid Buying More Than You Need

A very large container set may seem like good value, but unused containers still take up cabinet space.

Start with the sizes you actually need and expand your collection later if necessary.

14. Check Replacement Options

For some container systems, lids or individual containers may be available separately. This can be useful if one part becomes damaged or lost.

15. Think About Long-Term Use

Choose containers based on how well they fit your everyday routine, rather than choosing only because of appearance.

A simple set that is easy to clean, store and use regularly can be more practical than a large collection that stays in the cabinet.

How to Organize Food Storage Containers

Store containers and lids together whenever possible. If cabinet space is limited, stack containers by size and keep lids vertically in a dedicated section.

Remove damaged or rarely used containers occasionally so your storage area does not become overcrowded.

What Should You Check Before Buying?

Before purchasing a container set, consider these basic questions:

  • Do the sizes match the food you normally store?
  • Will they fit your refrigerator and cabinets?
  • Are the lids easy to use?
  • Are the cleaning instructions suitable for your routine?
  • Are microwave or dishwasher instructions clearly provided?
  • Can individual pieces or lids be replaced if needed?

Frequently Asked Questions

Are glass or plastic food containers better?

Neither material is automatically best for every household. Consider your storage needs, cleaning routine, weight preferences and the manufacturer's instructions for the product.

What container shape saves the most space?

Stackable rectangular or square containers can make efficient use of many refrigerator and cabinet shelves, although the best shape depends on your available storage space.

How many food storage containers do I need?

It depends on how often you prepare leftovers and how much food you normally store. A small selection of useful sizes is often enough to start.

Final Thoughts

Choosing food storage containers is mainly about finding a practical balance between size, material, lid design, cleaning and storage.

Instead of buying the largest set available, think about how you actually use your kitchen. The right containers should make everyday food storage simpler, not create another organization problem.
